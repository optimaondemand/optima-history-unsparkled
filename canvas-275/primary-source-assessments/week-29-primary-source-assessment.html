Primary Source Assessment

Read the sources below. Then answer both questions in 3 to 5 sentences each, in your own words. Use what you learned in the lessons as well as what the source says. Your teacher will score your answers with the rubric on this page.

SOURCE A
Isaac M. Cline, "Special Report on the Galveston Hurricane of September 8, 1900," Monthly Weather Review (excerpts)

[Telegram to the Central Office, Washington, morning of September 8, 1900:] Unusually heavy swells from the southeast, intervals of one to five minutes, overflowing low places south portion of city three to four blocks from beach.

[From the report:] The hurricane which visited Galveston Island on Saturday, September 8, 1900, was no doubt one of the most important meteorological events in the world's history. The ruin which it wrought beggars description, and conservative estimates place the loss of life at the appalling figure, 6,000.

SOURCE B
Houston Daily Post, January 11 and 12, 1901, headlines on the Beaumont gusher

January 11, 1901: OIL STRUCK NEAR BEAUMONT. A Stream of Petroleum Shot Into the Air for a Hundred Feet. Prospector Who Has Been at Work for Two Years Has His Faith Rewarded. The Flow Is Estimated at 5000 Barrels Per Day.

January 12, 1901: THE BIG OIL WELL. Said that the Capacity of It Is the Greatest in the World. Sixteen Thousand Barrels Daily Is the Estimate Made by Captain W. A. Fletcher.

QUESTION 1

Cline's morning telegram reports swells overflowing low places three to four blocks from the beach. His report counts about 6,000 dead. Using the lesson, explain how a storm that began with flooding of low places became the deadliest disaster in U.S. history.

QUESTION 2

The Post's estimate of the Beaumont well was 5,000 barrels a day on January 11 and 16,000 on January 12, while later accounts say about 100,000. What should a careful reader keep in mind about early newspaper reports as evidence?
