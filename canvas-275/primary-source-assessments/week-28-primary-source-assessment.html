SOURCE A
Grand State Farmers' Alliance of Texas, platform adopted at Cleburne, 1886 (excerpt)

We, the delegates to the Grand State Farmers' Alliance of Texas, in convention assembled at Cleburne, Johnson County, Texas, A.D. 1886, do hereby recommend and demand of our State and National governments, . . . such legislation as shall secure to our people freedom from the onerous and shameful abuses that the industrial classes are now suffering at the hands of arrogant capitalists and powerful corporations. We demand:

[from the list of demands:] That railroad property shall be assessed at the full nominal value of the stock on which the railroad seeks to declare a dividend.

QUESTION 1

The Alliance blames "arrogant capitalists and powerful corporations" and demands that the railroads be taxed at their full stock value. What idea about how government should treat big business is behind these demands, and how did Governor Hogg later act on a similar idea?

QUESTION 2

Suppose the Alliance had never formed a political party and had only kept running cooperatives. How might its goals, such as railroad regulation, have fared? Explain your reasoning.
