Primary Source Assessment

Read the sources below. Then answer both questions in 3 to 5 sentences each, in your own words. Use what you learned in the lessons as well as what the source says. Your teacher will score your answers with the rubric on this page.

SOURCE A
Hernandez v. Texas, 347 U.S. 475 (1954), opinion of Chief Justice Warren (excerpts)

The Fourteenth Amendment is not directed solely against discrimination due to a "two-class theory," that is, based upon differences between "white" and Negro.

But community prejudices are not static, and from time to time other differences from the community norm may define other groups which need the same protection.

SOURCE B
Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629 (1950), opinion of Chief Justice Vinson (excerpt)

[T]he University of Texas Law School possesses to a far greater degree those qualities which are incapable of objective measurement but which make for greatness in a law school. . . . reputation of the faculty, experience of the administration, position and influence of the alumni, standing in the community, traditions and prestige.

QUESTION 1

In Hernandez, the Court says that "community prejudices are not static." What idea about the Constitution and equal protection is behind this sentence, and what fact about Pete Hernandez's trial shows why it mattered?

QUESTION 2

In Sweatt, the Court looked at qualities such as reputation, alumni, and tradition, not only buildings and books. Suppose the Court had decided that the separate law school was equal. What might that have meant for later cases, including schools? Explain your reasoning.
